**California CARB compliance** is a label fleets hear constantly—but it is not one single checkbox. The **California Air Resources Board (CARB)** administers many rules affecting engines, fuels, and operations. For a large share of **heavy-duty truck operators**, an especially important piece today is the **Clean Truck Check** program and its online system, **CTC-VIS**.

This article frames **CARB compliance** the way most truck owners experience it: registrations, fees, testing windows, and enforcement risk. It is educational only. Carb Cert is a **third-party filing assistance** provider; we are **not CARB** and we do **not** perform emissions tests.

## CARB compliance in plain language

At a high level, **CARB compliance** means your vehicles and equipment meet the **regulatory requirements** that apply to how they are built, maintained, tested, and registered in California systems. Requirements differ by **vehicle type, model year, fuel, and how you use the truck** (intrastate vs. interstate, ports, etc.).

For heavy-duty trucks, compliance often touches:

- **Emissions-related inspections** required on a schedule (including under **Clean Truck Check** for in-scope vehicles).
- **Fees and certificates** tracked in **CTC-VIS** for that program.
- **Other CARB programs** that may apply to the same fleet (your compliance partner or counsel can help you map them).

Always treat **[CARB’s official publications](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/)** as the source of truth when a detail affects a legal or operational decision.

## Where Clean Truck Check sits in the stack

**Clean Truck Check** targets many **heavy-duty vehicles over 14,000 lbs GVWR**. If that describes your power units, you should already be thinking in terms of:

- **CTC-VIS registration** and up-to-date fleet data.
- **Periodic testing** at [CARB-authorized facilities](https://carbtestfinder.com/carb-testers).
- **Annual compliance fees** set under the program.

Missing any of those elements can trigger **DMV registration holds**, **roadside enforcement**, or **access problems** at certain facilities—even when the truck “runs fine” day to day.

## California CARB compliance vs. “just passing smog”

Operators sometimes assume a **passing emissions test** at any shop is enough. Under Clean Truck Check, **only authorized testing pathways count** for program compliance, and **CTC-VIS** must reflect results and fees correctly. Treat unauthorized shortcuts as **high risk**. Tester inspection fees are set by the shop and are separate from CARB’s annual fee; [what a CARB emissions test typically costs](https://carbtestfinder.com/blog/carb-emissions-test-cost) breaks those line items apart.

## Fees, certificates, and paperwork

CARB sets **compliance fees** associated with the program. The dollar amount and due timing can change; verify on CARB before you budget. A **certificate of compliance** (or equivalent program status) is what many operators need to show they are current for a given vehicle year.

## Fleet practices that reduce risk

Strong fleets usually do the following:

- **Centralize vehicle data** (VIN, year, fuel type, plate, last test, next due date).
- **Assign one owner** for CARB deadlines across states and business units.
- **Coordinate with your shop** so testing appointments align with CTC-VIS due dates—not after enforcement windows close.
- **Re-check rules annually**; CARB updates can shift dates and requirements.
- **Track federal filings next to CARB ones.** Interstate carriers also renew [Unified Carrier Registration (UCR)](https://renewucrregistration.com/what-is-ucr) every year—a separate obligation from Clean Truck Check that is easy to forget on the same calendar.
