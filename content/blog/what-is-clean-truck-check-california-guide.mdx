If you run **heavy-duty vehicles in or into California**, you have probably heard the phrase **Clean Truck Check**. It is one of the state’s main programs for keeping high-emission trucks accountable through periodic testing, registration in an official system, and compliance fees. This guide explains what the program is, who it applies to, and how **CTC-VIS** (the Clean Truck Check Vehicle Inspection System) fits into your workflow.

Carb Cert provides **third-party reporting assistance** through CTC-VIS. We are **not** the California Air Resources Board (CARB) and **not** an emissions testing lab. Always confirm current requirements on the official [CARB website](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/).

## Who has to worry about Clean Truck Check?

Clean Truck Check generally applies to **heavy-duty vehicles with a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) over 14,000 pounds** that operate in California. That includes many Class 4 and larger trucks, tractors, and some larger commercial vehicles. **Fleets, owner-operators, and out-of-state carriers** that enter California with qualifying trucks typically need to participate—not only companies headquartered in the state.

If you are unsure whether a specific unit is in scope, use CARB’s official resources or speak with a qualified compliance advisor. Rules and exemptions can change; this article is for general education only.

## What does “compliance” actually mean?

For most operators, compliance involves a few recurring themes:

- **Registering the vehicle** in **CTC-VIS** and keeping account information current.
- **Completing emissions-related testing** on the schedule CARB assigns (for example, based on model year and equipment). Testing is performed at **CARB-authorized facilities**—not by filing services.
- **Paying the annual compliance fee** CARB sets for the program (amounts and due dates should be verified on CARB’s site).

Failing to stay on schedule can lead to **registration holds, enforcement at roadside, port access issues**, and other headaches that stop trucks from earning revenue.

## How CTC-VIS fits in

**CTC-VIS** is the online system CARB uses for much of the Clean Truck Check workflow: vehicle records, test results, fees, and certificates of compliance. Think of it as the **system of record** for the program’s administrative side.

Many operators work with their **shop or testing provider** for the actual inspection, then need to make sure results and fees are reflected correctly in CTC-VIS. That is where confusion—and missed deadlines—often happens.

Carb Cert helps with **third-party reporting and filing assistance** in CTC-VIS so owners and fleets can focus on dispatch and maintenance instead of wrestling with account steps. We still remind every customer: **emissions testing itself must be done at an authorized facility**, and **we are not affiliated with CARB**.

## Testing timelines: plan ahead

CARB has published phased timelines for testing frequency (for example, semi-annual versus quarterly) depending on vehicle characteristics. **Deadlines are easy to miss** when you are juggling multiple units across states.

Practical tips:

- **Build a vehicle list** with VIN, model year, fuel type, and last test date.
- **Set calendar reminders** well before CARB due dates.
- **Book [authorized testing](https://carbtestfinder.com/carb-testers) early**—shops get busy before quarter-end.

If this is a first inspection, [how to prepare for a CARB emissions test](https://carbtestfinder.com/blog/carb-emissions-test-prep-checklist) covers documents, vehicle readiness, and common delay causes.

For the authoritative schedule, rely on CARB—not on any third-party blog, including this one.

## Out-of-state trucks

A common myth is that **non-California plates** exempt you from Clean Truck Check. In practice, **if your heavy-duty vehicle operates in California**, you should assume you need to comply with registration and testing rules unless CARB documentation clearly says otherwise for your situation. Treat interstate compliance as part of your lane planning, not an afterthought.

## Clean Truck Check vs. other CARB programs

California has several **heavy-duty and emissions programs** (for example, broader CARB truck rules, transport refrigeration units, and more). Clean Truck Check is **one specific program** focused on periodic testing and related fees for in-scope vehicles. Your legal or compliance advisor can map which programs apply to your fleet.

## Key takeaways

- **Clean Truck Check** is CARB’s program for periodic testing, fees, and compliance certification for many heavy-duty vehicles over **14,000 lbs GVWR**.
- **CTC-VIS** is the official vehicle and fee system operators must use for much of the process.
- **Testing** only counts when done at **CARB-authorized** facilities.
- **Out-of-state operators** often must comply when running in California.
- Use **[CARB](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/)** for binding rules; use service providers (like us) for **administrative help**—not legal advice.
